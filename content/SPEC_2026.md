# TMUA 2026 specification: points that matter for writing questions

Source: UAT-UK, *TMUA Content Specification, for assessment in October 2026 and January 2027*.

## Format
* Two 75-minute papers, 20 multiple-choice questions each, taken one after the other. No calculator,
  no formula booklet, no penalty for wrong answers. Computer-based at Pearson VUE centres since 2024.
* Option count: published pre-2024 papers use A–H. Computer-based (2024+) papers are reported by
  candidates/tutors as mostly five options (A–E), with longer lists for statement-combination
  questions. No 2024/2025 papers have been released. **House rule: use 5 options as the norm; use
  6–8 only for "which of I, II, III" combination questions or where the structure genuinely needs it.**
* Candidates report 2024/2025 questions as wordier and more applied: the same maths wrapped in a
  short scenario to translate first. UAT-UK says content and style are unchanged. **House rule:
  about a third of questions per topic use a short real-world or scenario framing where it fits
  naturally.**

## Explicit exclusions / rules (do not violate)
* **Change of base formula for logarithms: "Questions requiring knowledge of the change of base
  formula will not be set."** No question may need it (including log_4 x ↔ log_2 x conversions).
* **Points of inflexion will not be examined** (a qualitative understanding of them in simple
  polynomial curves is expected). Don't ask about them directly.
* Differentiation from first principles is excluded.
* **Formulae for spheres, pyramids and cones are given if needed**: state them in the question.
* Paper 2 logic: **candidates are not expected to recognise or use symbolic notation** (⇒, ∧, ∨, ¬,
  ∀, ∃) **nor complete formal truth tables.** Questions must be in words. Solutions may use a
  symbol occasionally for brevity but should read in words.
* √x always means the positive square root.

## Paper 1 / Section 1 content (AS pure + higher GCSE)
MM1 algebra: indices, surds/rationalising, quadratics (discriminant, completing the square),
simultaneous (linear + quadratic), linear & quadratic inequalities, polynomials (expansion,
factorisation, division by linear AND quadratic, factor & remainder theorems), many-to-one vs
one-to-one functions, √x and |x|.
MM2 sequences: nth term, recurrences x_{n+1}=f(x_n), arithmetic series, finite geometric sum,
sum to infinity with |r|<1, binomial (1+x)^n and (a+f(x))^n, n!, nCr.
MM3 coordinates: lines (both forms), parallel/perpendicular, circles (both forms), circle
properties a–g (perpendicular bisects chord, tangent ⟂ radius, centre angle = 2×circumference,
semicircle, same segment, cyclic quad, alternate segment).
MM4 trig: sine/cosine rules incl. ambiguous case, problems in 2 OR 3 dimensions, area ½ab sin C,
radians incl. arc, sector AND segment area, exact values, graphs/symmetry/periodicity,
tan = sin/cos, sin²+cos²=1, equations in intervals, e.g. sin²(2x+π/3)=½ on −2π<x<2π,
12cos²x+6sin x−10=2 on 0°<x<360°.
MM5 exp/log: y=a^x graphs, log laws incl. log(1/x) = −log x, log_a a = 1; equations a^x=b and
reducible ones e.g. 3^{2x}=4, 25^x − 3·5^x + 2 = 0. (NO change of base.)
MM6 differentiation: x^n for rational n incl. simplification first (e.g. (3x−2)²/(x√x)),
second derivatives, rates of change, tangents/normals, stationary points (max/min only),
strictly increasing/decreasing.
MM7 integration: definite integral vs area (difference must be understood), x^n rational n
incl. simplification first, **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus incl. d/dx ∫_a^x f(t)dt = f(x)**,
**combining integrals over equal or contiguous ranges**, **trapezium rule + over/under-estimate**,
**solving dy/dx = f(x)**.
MM8 graphs: lines, quadratics, cubics, trig, log, exponential, square-root and **modulus**
graphs; transformations af(x), f(x)+a, f(x+a), f(ax) and compositions, f(g(x)); effect of m, c;
y=a(x+b)²+c; shape via differentiation; intersections with axes, possible numbers of real roots
of polynomials; intersections of graphs ↔ simultaneous equations.

GCSE part (M1–M7): units & compound measures (speed, density, pressure, rates); number
(primes, HCF/LCM, unique factorisation, systematic listing/product rule, standard form,
**recurring decimals ↔ fractions**, **upper and lower bounds**, estimation); ratio, proportion
(**direct and inverse proportion incl. powers**), percentages, **growth/decay and iterative
processes**; algebra (rearranging, identities, sequences incl. quadratic nth term); geometry
(angles, polygons, congruence, similarity incl. area/volume scale, transformations,
**vectors incl. geometric proof**, Pythagoras in 2D/3D, circle theorems, plans/elevations,
**bearings**, arcs/sectors, surface area/volume); **statistics** (mean/median/mode/range,
grouped-data estimates, **histograms incl. frequency density**, **cumulative frequency**,
quartiles/IQR, comparing distributions, scatter/correlation); probability (tables, trees, Venn,
conditional probability, independent vs dependent).

## Paper 2 / Section 2
Arg1 true/false; and, or (inclusive), not; "if A then B", "A if B", "A only if B", "A if and only
if B"; converse; contrapositive; relationship between truth of a statement, its converse and
contrapositive. Arg2 necessary / sufficient. Arg3 for all, for some (= at least one), there
exists. Arg4 negating statements using any of these.
Prf1 direct proof, proof by cases, contradiction, disproof by counterexample (follow, and in
simple cases construct). Prf2 deduce implications from given statements. **Prf3 conjectures from
small cases, then justify.** **Prf4 rearrange statements into the correct order of a proof.**
Prf5 problems needing a sophisticated chain of reasoning.
Err1 identifying errors in purported proofs. Err2 common errors, e.g. "if ab = ac then b = c",
"if sin A = sin B then A = B".
