# Exam strategy

The TMUA is two 75-minute papers of 20 multiple-choice questions each, taken one after the other on screen at a Pearson VUE centre, with no calculator and no formula sheet. For 2026 it is also the admissions test for Oxford and Imperial maths and computer science, so the field is strong. Wrong answers cost nothing. Your raw marks are converted to a scale from 1.0 to 9.0. That leaves three levers: get the doable questions right, don't bleed time, and never leave a blank.

## The clock

75 minutes for 20 questions is **3 minutes 45 seconds each on average**. Questions get harder through a paper on the whole, but not strictly, and a hard question 6 is worth the same as an easy question 19.

- **Checkpoints:** aim to be past Q5 at 19 minutes, Q10 at 38, Q15 at 56. The timed modes in this app show the same pace line.
- **The 90-second rule:** if you have no plan after 90 seconds, pick your best guess, flag it, and move on. Come back only once the rest is done.
- **Never leave a blank.** With five options a blind guess is worth a fifth of a mark. Knock out two and it's a third.

> **Exam tip:** Do the whole paper in one pass, answering and flagging as you go. Spend the final 5–10 minutes on flagged questions, most promising first.

## Before you calculate, look at the options

The options are information. TMUA answers are designed to come out cleanly, so check them before doing algebra.

- **Eliminate by sign, size or form.** If the answer must be positive, cross out the negatives. If it must be irrational, cross out the integers.
- **Test a special value.** For "which expression equals…" questions, put $x = 0$, $x = 1$ or $n = 1$ into the question and into each option.
- **Work backwards.** For "which value of $k$…" questions, it's often quicker to substitute each option than to solve.
- **Look at how the options differ.** If two options differ only by a factor of 2, or by $<$ versus $\le$, the question is testing exactly that detail.

> **Tip:** In this app, right-click an option (or press Shift + its letter) to cross it out. Use elimination on every question.

## Translate the wording first

Recent papers are reported to be wordier: the same mathematics wrapped in a short scenario. Before any algebra, write the bare question in one line: the unknown, the condition, and what is being asked ("number of solutions", "largest value", "which must be true"). Underline every *not*, *only*, *exactly*, *at least*, and every interval and endpoint.

## Sketch first

A quick sketch settles a lot of questions: number of solutions, sign of an integral, when an inequality holds, which graph is which. Sketch before any algebra about intersections, roots, maxima or areas.

## Paper 2 habits

- **"Which of I, II, III is true":** judge each statement on its own, then find the option that matches. Once you know one statement's truth value, cross out every option that disagrees and skip what you no longer need.
- **"Must be true" / "could be true":** hunt for counterexamples. A single example kills "must", and a single example proves "could".
- **Necessary and sufficient:** "P is sufficient for Q" means P ⇒ Q. "P is necessary for Q" means Q ⇒ P. Write the arrow down every time.
- **Errors in proofs:** go line by line, asking "does this follow from the lines above?" Check divisions (by zero?), square roots (±?), squaring (extra roots?) and inequalities multiplied by possibly negative quantities.

## Common ways to lose easy marks

- Answering a different question: "the sum of the roots" when it asked for "the product", or "area" when it asked for "the integral".
- Dropping a case: $x^2 = 9$ has two roots, and $|x| < 3$ is an interval.
- Endpoints: strict versus non-strict inequalities, and solutions at the ends of an interval.
- Missing "not" and "false" in the question. Underline them.

## The final day

- No new topics. Read *Facts to know cold*, do one light timed set, then stop.
- Check your test centre, time, and ID requirements the day before.
- Sleep matters more than one more paper.

## In the test

- Read each question once, carefully, and underline the target on your working sheet.
- Keep your working tidy enough to recheck in 20 seconds.
- If a question rattles you, flag it and start the next one fresh. One bad question costs one mark; carrying it into the next three costs more.
